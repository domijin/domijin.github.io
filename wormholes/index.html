How does geometry enter an answer?

The Euclidean path integral sums over geometries satisfying boundary conditions. The semiclassical approximation organises contributions around saddles, commonly with an exponential factor e−IE/ħ. This is related to Euclidean methods used to describe quantum tunnelling.[1]

02

BABY UNIVERSES

What if some degrees of freedom lie outside our description?

Baby universes are a theoretical construction in quantum gravity: closed spatial branches can enter the description as additional degrees of freedom. Unlike Schwinger pair production of charged particles inside a fixed spacetime, gravitational tunnelling changes the spatial topology itself.

In some models, treating these degrees of freedom can be reformulated as averaging over effective coupling parameters. α sectors distinguish fixing one theory from averaging many theories; parameters within a given sector do not jump randomly over time.[1]

A familiar distinction

One coin, or a bag of different coins

Repeatedly tossing one coin gives random outcomes within a fixed model. Drawing from a bag of coins with different biases and then averaging introduces differences between models. This is an analogy for two kinds of averaging, not a physical model of baby universes.

Holographic duality lets gravity and a boundary quantum theory test each other. For a BTZ black hole in three-dimensional anti-de Sitter space (AdS₃), high-energy state counting in the boundary two-dimensional conformal field theory reproduces the leading black-hole entropy. Assumptions, units and range of validity

We use c = 3ℓ/(2G₃) and M = r₊²/(8G₃ℓ²), where ℓ is the AdS curvature radius and G₃ the three-dimensional Newton constant. For a two-dimensional CFT with large central charge and a sufficiently sparse low-energy spectrum, the leading Cardy entropy is universal over an extended high-energy regime. The slider uses E/c ≥ 1, on the high-energy side of that discussion. Finite-c values are illustrative; omitted corrections have not been estimated. The negative cosmological constant and boundary conditions of AdS cannot simply be transferred to a de Sitter description of our universe.[3]

ENSEMBLES & FACTORISATION

Multiply first, or average first?

Two non-interacting boundary systems should factorise within a fixed theory. If a gravitational calculation includes wormholes connecting the boundaries, their compatibility with this expectation needs explaining. Ensemble averaging offers one interpretation: dependence on a shared parameter can make the average of a product differ from the product of averages.[4]

From state counting to interactions

OPE coefficients describe how operators combine in a CFT. Appropriate averages of heavy operators admit universal asymptotic formulae in particular limits. Universality constrains statistics; it does not determine every individual coefficient.[5]

Further research constructs consistency-constrained ensembles of CFT data, matching some semiclassical three-dimensional gravitational calculations to ensemble averages, including multi-boundary Euclidean wormholes. The three-member model above explains averaging only; it does not implement these OPE formulae.[6]

Questions that remain open

What exactly are we averaging?

For general gravitational theories, the ensemble's members and measure, factorisation within a fixed theory, and extension to de Sitter each require further arguments. Success in particular models does not establish that all gravity must be an ensemble average.

WHAT THIS HELPS US ASK

What might wormholes help us understand?

In this research, Euclidean wormholes help test theories: they organise connected contributions to the gravitational path integral, probe black-hole microstate statistics and raise questions about compatibility between gravity and boundary quantum theories. Their potential value lies in understanding quantum gravity, rather than a demonstrated engineering application.
