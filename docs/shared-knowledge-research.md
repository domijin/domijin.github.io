# Shared knowledge: research and editorial boundaries

Research notes · 17 November 2026. These inform an illustrative interface; they do not validate its effectiveness.

## Connection to the existing writing

- [Free Market in a Possibility Universe & The Collaborative Manifesto](../articles/blog/free-market-in-a-possibility-universe.md), September 2019: the author links reasoning and a shared knowledge base to his collaborative-society ambition. This is the strongest direct bridge; avoid inventing a new governance architecture around it.
- [Overachiever’s Responsibility](../articles/substack/overachievers-responsibility-reflection.md), February 2025: alignment of priorities and complementary skills. Its GCD/LCM language is a metaphor, not a mathematical model of people.
- [Serendipity](../articles/substack/serendipity-where-it-sparks.md), December 2024: different paths through fields and relationships can inform how people understand one another.
- [Open Learning](../articles/blog/open-learning.md): a working hypothesis for open knowledge, individual mastery, voluntary collaboration and examinable contributions. Four proposed courses develop Purpose, Potentials, Communication and Resilience; Informative, Interactive and Adaptive remain a separate dimension.

The new page belongs between Human Agency and Working Together. It shows one proposed mechanism within the wider vision: people organise and share knowledge without becoming interchangeable or surrendering judgement. It is not a comprehensive account of a society or a validated learning programme.

## Primary sources consulted

### Grounding in communication

Herbert H. Clark and Susan E. Brennan (1991), *Grounding in Communication*, in *Perspectives on Socially Shared Cognition*, pp. 127–149. [Author-hosted PDF](https://web.stanford.edu/~clark/1990s/Clark%2C%20H.H.%20_%20Brennan%2C%20S.E.%20_Grounding%20in%20communication_%201991.pdf).

Consulted the original chapter, particularly the opening account of coordinating content and process and the discussion of grounding criteria and media. Shared vocabulary does not establish mutual understanding. Design inference: show explanation, interpretation and acknowledgement as distinct actions. A button in this demonstration cannot establish another real person's understanding.

### Information that groups fail to share

Garold Stasser and William Titus (1985), *Pooling of Unshared Information in Group Decision Making: Biased Information Sampling During Discussion*, JPSP 48(6), 1467–1478. [Original paper](https://www.uni-muenster.de/imperia/md/content/psyifp/aeechterhoff/vorlesungkommunikation/stasser_titus_unsharedinfogroupdisc_jpsp1985.pdf).

Consulted the original paper, including its political-caucus simulation and opening results. Groups in this task failed to combine potentially corrective information and discussion favoured shared information and existing preferences. Design inference: make distinctive evidence inspectable alongside common topics. This does not establish that every disagreement is useful or that a visual map fixes information-sharing bias.

### Generating an explanation together

Michelene T. H. Chi and Ruth Wylie (2014), *The ICAP Framework: Linking Cognitive Engagement to Active Learning Outcomes*, Educational Psychologist 49(4), 219–243. [Author-institution PDF](https://csi.asu.edu/wp-content/uploads/2018/01/ChiWylie2014ICAP.pdf), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/00461520.2014.965823).

Consulted the original framework and its stated caveats. It differentiates forms of cognitive engagement and reviews associated learning evidence. Design inference: invite a reconstruction or question rather than merely showing two overlapping shapes. Moving a slider, revealing text or sharing a topic does not demonstrate durable learning. No learning-gain multiplier or mastery score follows from this demonstration.

### Additional lead, not used as verified support

Daniel M. Wegner (1987), *Transactive Memory: A Contemporary Analysis of the Group Mind*. [Publisher record](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-1-4612-4634-3_9). Only the publisher preview was accessible in this research pass. Do not base detailed claims on its full text without reading it.

## Visual and epistemic contract

- All people, topic maps and example observations are fictional or synthetic and labelled accordingly.
- A circle of competence is a schematic, topic-specific region, not a psychometric score or a measured inventory of a mind. Node positions and region areas carry no quantitative meaning.
- Common topic, checked understanding, shared goal and agreement on a claim are different states.
- A newly encountered idea is not acquired competence. An unanswered joint question is not a discovery. A provisional contribution can retain disagreement and uncertainty.
- The two people keep distinct provenance: experience, source, assumption and unknown. Disclosure is voluntary.
- Alignment is on a purpose and method. Agreement is neither required nor evidence of truth.
- No AI inference, account, profiling, actual publication or network model download is required for the core demonstration.
- Retain a useful static initial map and readable explanation without JavaScript; all controls must be labelled and keyboard accessible.

## Editorial handling

Raw prompts are private reference material outside the repository. They must not appear in the post, Markdown, reader bundle or retained public build chunks. Published diagrams use Mermaid-rendered SVG assets, with source files available separately rather than raw code displayed as a figure.
