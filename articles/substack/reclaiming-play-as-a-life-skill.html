Chapter III · Human Agency, Older People’s Independence and Lifelong Play

🌱 Reclaiming Play as a Life Skill

By Domi Jin (Dongming Jin) · March 2025

🎮 What Games Really Teach Us — Especially As We Age

I recently came across a short article that really stuck with me. It shared the results of a study conducted in Turkey, where a group of seniors living in care homes were invited to play simple recreational games — bingo, word puzzles, hot-cold guessing games, matching cards — twice a week for two months.

The outcome? The group that played games reported significantly higher happiness, life satisfaction, and lower feelings of loneliness and somatization (physical symptoms triggered by emotional distress).

“Recreational games may give psychological benefits to older adults. Yet, results of the study need to be interpreted with caution and need to be replicated.” (Source: The effect of recreational games on happiness, life satisfaction, loneliness, and somatisation in elderly individuals: a non-randomized controlled trial, BMC Psychology, 2024)

That got me thinking: Maybe games are far more than just entertainment. Maybe they hold deep, untapped potential — especially when it comes to living well and aging well.

🧠 What Is a Game, Really?

Game designer and futurist Jane McGonigal, in her book Reality Is Broken, defines a game as having four key elements:

A goal – a specific outcome players work to achieve -

Rules – constraints that push us to be creative and strategic -

A feedback system – progress indicators that keep us motivated -

Voluntary participation – we play because we choose to

She offers a powerful definition:

“A game is the voluntary attempt to overcome unnecessary obstacles.”

In other words, we play games not to escape reality, but to feel more engaged, more in control, more fulfilled.

🌱 Reclaiming Play as a Life Skill

This post isn’t about challenging stereotypes around video games. It’s about inviting a different way of thinking:

What if we all embraced the best parts of games — clarity, challenge, feedback, and voluntary action — as tools to live more fully?

For seniors, games aren’t childish distractions. They’re opportunities to connect, engage, and grow — whether it’s through puzzles, investing, learning a new tech, or mentoring someone younger.

And for all of us, embracing that “gameful” mindset could mean the difference between feeling stuck and feeling in flow.

So here’s to playing the long game — intentionally, joyfully, and with purpose.a scene where seniors enjoy playing bingo

👵👴 我与长者们的一次对话

我把这项研究分享给我接触的几位长者朋友，他们的反馈很真实：

“大学毕业的精英与没念过大学的老人，退休后的休闲程度会有很大区别。文化程度高的人中，闲得无聊只好玩游戏的人不多，对高科技、AI、医疗这些更感兴趣。”

这段话让我意识到一个有趣的观点： 看似“高科技”、“AI”、“投资”这些高阶兴趣，和游戏，其实并不冲突，甚至可以看作是另一种层次的‘社会游戏’（Social Game）。

💼 理财、健康管理、技术学习，何尝不是一种“社会游戏”？

退休之后，有人选择打牌、种花、旅行，也有人喜欢研究投资组合、AI 工具、医疗前沿。 这些看似“严肃”的兴趣，其实也是在玩一种复杂的社会游戏：

信息获取 → 谁知道的更早、更深 -

资源整合 → 时间、注意力、金钱、人脉 -

策略制定 → 什么时候买、什么时候退出 -

身份感与成就感 → 你是谁，在这个社会系统中扮演什么角色

每个人都在玩，只是“游戏的形态”不同而已。
