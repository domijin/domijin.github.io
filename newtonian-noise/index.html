COHERENT VS INCOHERENT ACOUSTIC FIELDS · EQS. (5) & (10)

Infrasonic Newtonian noise and underground wave-packet floors

A perfectly coherent horizontal sound wave at speed v_s = 340 m/s is exponentially suppressed underground as e^(−ωD/v_s). Real atmospheric wave trains, however, stay spatially coherent only over a finite packet length l = Qλ, replacing pure exponential decay below e^(−πQ) with a weaker power-law tail.

ADVECTED KOLMOGOROV TURBULENCE & 1D MONTE CARLO

Why turbulent wind turns an exponential cliff into a power law

Smooth uniform wind glides past a mirror over timescale D/v, dying off exponentially above f ~ v/(2πD). Once surface roughness or eddies roll air parcels along cycloidal loops of radius R = v/Ω, frozen temperature pockets re-accelerate near f = v/(2πR), lifting a steep f^(−29/6) locus into the gravitational-wave band.

SURFACE ENCLOSURES D_EFF & VOLUMETRIC TOMOGRAPHY · §§2.3 & 5

Building a bigger hall versus mapping every cubic metre of wind

HEURISTIC SURFACE SHELTER STANDOFF D_EFF = ¼⟨r⁻¹⟩⁻¹

Because surface structures surround the test mass on three sides rather than only from above, the inverse-distance solid-angle integral compresses effective depth:

Paper ratio D_eff6.58 m
Uniform upper-hemisphere quadrature7.22 m

For a hemisphere of radius R = 6 m, D_eff = 3.0 m matches the roll-off of Fiorucci et al. (2018). For a half-cube, Wang & Creighton quote 0.5480 h whereas uniform upper-hemisphere numerical quadrature evaluates to 0.6015 h; either way, achieving D_eff = 100 m via an above-ground hangar would require a ~180 m structure, making underground siting far more practical (while simultaneously cutting seismic NN).

WHY ACTIVE THERMAL SUBTRACTION IS HARD · N ~ (2πf D / v)³

Coherent seismic and acoustic fields can be predicted from a sparse perimeter array. Incoherent thermal eddies have correlation length l_corr ~ v / ω and demand real-time 3D tomography throughout a hemisphere of radius D:

Independent 3D volumetric parcels per snapshot2.48e+5

Eddy correlation scale l = v/(2πf) = 1.59 m at v = 10 m/s. Achieving 100 m underground performance on the surface at 1 Hz requires ~10⁵–10⁶ independent sub-second temperature measurements to millikelvin precision—likely achievable only via remote atmospheric lidar or absorption-line tomography, placing solar-orbit missions in view for <1 Hz bands.

WHAT THIS CHANGES FOR OBSERVATORIES

Why thermal advection sets the low-frequency frontier

Coherent seismic and acoustic fluctuations can in principle be monitored with sparse seismometers or infrasound arrays and subtracted in feed-forward pipelines. Wind-advected temperature perturbations, however, carry correlation scales of only a couple of metres at 1 Hz—meaning atmospheric Newtonian noise acts as an irreducible siting constraint unless detectors bury test masses at least ~100 m underground (for 2–10 Hz sensitivity) or leave Earth's atmosphere behind in solar orbit below 1 Hz.
