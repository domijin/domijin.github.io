Mission

Exploring how technology can expand human potential and empower people to achieve more together.

Background

My journey began with fundamental science, studying the universe through computational astrophysics, numerical simulations, and statistical modeling (Ph.D. in Applied Physics & Astrophysics, UT Arlington; LSST Data Science Fellow).

I subsequently moved into applied machine learning, building decision systems for consumer products, marketplaces, and advertising technology.

Entrepreneurship brought a different perspective: understanding what people actually need, how they make decisions, and why technically promising solutions sometimes fail in the real world.

Across these experiences, one question has remained central:

How can we use technology to help individuals become more capable and enable people to collaborate more effectively?

Philosophy

I believe humanity’s greatest achievements emerge through collaboration.

Intelligence matters, but so do communication, trust, complementary capabilities, incentives, and the freedom to make meaningful choices.

Technology is a means rather than an end. Its value should ultimately be measured by how it improves people’s opportunities, autonomy, relationships, and quality of life.

My approach combines scientific curiosity with practical experimentation: challenge assumptions, build working systems, measure outcomes, learn from failure, and revise our understanding.

Intellectual Interests

My interests span several interconnected areas:

  • Collective Intelligence: How do individual capabilities combine to produce collective outcomes?
  • Human–AI Collaboration: How can intelligent systems augment human judgment and agency?
  • Organizational Intelligence: How do communication, coordination, trust, and incentives shape collaboration?
  • Complex Adaptive Systems: How do interactions among individuals give rise to emergent social and organizational behavior?
  • Decision Intelligence: How can information, feedback, and experimentation improve decisions?
  • Human Potential: How can technology make learning, opportunity, and meaningful participation more accessible?
  • AI and Society: How might increasingly capable intelligence change the ways we work, cooperate, and live?

These are continuing intellectual interests, not claims of completed research or established scientific findings.
